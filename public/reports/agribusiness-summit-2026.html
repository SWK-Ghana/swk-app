Agribusiness Summit 2026 · Event report

We came to build from the ground up.

On Saturday 7 November 2026, young people, farmers, founders and partners met in Accra for SWK Ghana's first in-person Summit. They heard from people who run agribusinesses, mapped businesses of their own, and left with a next step. This is what happened.

  • Saturday, 7 November 2026 · 9:00 AM to 2:00 PM
  • The GracedLife Leadership Centre, Ashaley Botwe, Accra
  • Theme: From the Ground Up · free to attend

Figures come from the Summit's registration form and the door check-in. Of the 138 registrations, 123 came in online before the day and 15 at the entrance. Sixty-one people took part on the day: 48 were checked in at the door, and three speakers, eight members of the SWK Ghana team, one volunteer and our photographer did not go through check-in. The day

Five hours, from the first welcome to the pledge.

The programme moved from listening to doing: hear from people in the sector, see what is working in the field, then sit down and map a business of your own.

Panel

Beyond the Farm Gate

Building profitable agribusinesses across the value chain

Three founders and practitioners took questions on where the money and the gaps really are: post-harvest loss and logistics, the slow uptake of technology on farms, and the room there is for people who never studied agriculture.

  • Elizabeth Kabukie Ocansey · moderator
  • Wilfred Agbi, Founder and CEO, Calidad Farms Limited

Workshop

One page, one business: the Agribusiness Model Canvas

Leticia Boateng, Digital Learning Advisor at GIZ's Agri-Business Facility for Africa, introduced the free Agribusiness e-Academy and then led the hands-on part of the day with Ernestina Gyamfi of SWK Ghana. Working in groups, participants mapped an agribusiness on a single page: customers, costs, partners and the first sale. Groups then shared their Canvases with the room.

What we learned

Three things we are taking into the next one.

A first event teaches more than it proves. This is what we will keep and what we will change.

01

The demand is there

138 people registered for a first in-person Summit, 60 of them already working in agribusiness, and 15 of them signed up at the door on the day. Young people are looking for this.

02

Registering is not arriving

Of the 123 people who registered online, 33 checked in on the day. Next time we will confirm seats in the final week, share travel directions earlier, and plan for the people who walk in.

03

Guard the clock, and the follow-up

Early feedback rated the content highly and asked us to keep better time. Mentorship was among the things attendees wanted most, so we have opened mentorship requests with our speakers.
