Why this matters

The idea is not the hard part. Being understood is.

Africa has the highest rate of entrepreneurship in the world, and more than three-quarters of African youth plan to start a business within five years. Yet capital and networks stay stubbornly out of reach — not always for want of a good idea, but for want of a clear one.

Investors lean on credentialed trust, using elite education and global networks as shorthand for lower risk. That creates a two-speed ecosystem. One practical lever closes part of that gap: the ability to explain what you do, clearly and credibly, in the time it takes to ride an elevator.

The research is blunt about it. Pitch quality measurably shifts investment decisions — and not only the content, but the delivery: tone, expression, word choice. This workshop treats the pitch as a skill you practise, not a talent you either have or lack.

The framework

Clear. Concise. Compelling.

The session's backbone is the Three C’s. You will build your pitch against them live, then test it on people who will tell you honestly whether it landed.

C

Clear

Plain language, no jargon. Who you are, what you do, and why it matters — with nothing left for the listener to decode.

C

Concise

Brevity is not rushing; it is ruthless prioritisation. Thirty to ninety seconds, carrying only what is genuinely essential.

C

Compelling

It has to leave them wanting more. A specific hook, a real story, or a blunt statement of the problem you are solving.
