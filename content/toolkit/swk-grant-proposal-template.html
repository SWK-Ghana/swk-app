Grant Proposal Template and Guide

A section-by-section structure most funders recognise, with prompts for what to write in each part, a checklist for before you submit, and the mistakes that sink most proposals.

Free to use and adapt For anyone writing a grant application swkghana.org/support · October 2026

How to use it

  • Always follow the funder's own form first. Use this to fill the gaps.
  • Copy the headings into your document and answer each prompt.
  • Word counts are guides. Keep to the funder's limits.

The golden rule

Write for a busy reader who knows nothing about you. Every section should answer one question: why should this funder back this work, now?

1 The proposal, section by section

Cover page 1 page

Project title, your legal name and registration number, contact person, amount requested, project length, location, and a one-paragraph summary (around 100 words).

Executive summary 200–300 words

The whole proposal in miniature. Write it last.

  • The problem and who it affects
  • What you will do, and what will change as a result
  • Why you are the right organisation, and how much it costs

About your organisation 200–400 words

Mission, years active, people reached so far (with numbers), relevant past projects, partners, and how you are governed. Funders look for evidence that you can deliver and account for their money.

The problem 300–500 words

  • What is the problem, and how do you know? Cite data, for example from the Ghana Statistical Service, and your own experience.
  • Who is affected, where, and how many people?
  • What causes it, what has been tried, and why is now the moment to act?

Who will take part 150–300 words

Who the project is for, how many people, how you will reach and select them, and how they helped shape the project. Say how you will include women, people with disabilities and others who are often left out.

Goal and objectives 100–200 words

One overall goal, then two to four objectives that are specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound. For example: "By June 2027, 200 young farmers in the Eastern Region will have a written business plan."

3 Before you submit

  • You meet every eligibility rule, and you have read the funder's priorities.
  • Every question is answered, within the word limits.
  • The budget adds up, and matches the narrative.
  • Names, numbers and dates are the same in every section.
  • Attachments ready: registration certificates, NPO licence, audited accounts, board list, key policies, letters of support.
  • Someone outside the project has read it for clarity.
  • Submitted a day early, in the right format and time zone.
Why proposals fail. Ignoring what the funder says it funds. A vague problem with no evidence. Activities that don't lead to the promised results. A budget that doesn't match the plan. Missing the deadline. Rejected? Ask politely for feedback, and use it to make the next one stronger.
