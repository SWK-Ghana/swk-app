Monitoring and Evaluation Starter Kit

Show what changed because of your work, not just what you did. A one-page theory of change, a results framework, indicator sheets and a simple learning rhythm for small teams.

Free to use and adapt For programme leads and M&E focal persons swkghana.org/support · October 2026

Three words to get right

  • Outputs are what you deliver: 4 workshops, 120 people trained.
  • Outcomes are what changes for people: 60% started saving.
  • Impact is the long-term change you contribute to.

Start small

Pick three to five indicators you will actually use. A small set measured well beats a long list nobody has time to collect.

1 Your theory of change, on one page

Fill each box left to right. Then read it back as a sentence: if we do these activities, then these outputs follow, so that these outcomes happen, contributing to this impact.

Assumptions

What must be true for each step to lead to the next? For example: "trained farmers can get the seed they need." Write them down, then check them as you go.

4 Collect data people can trust

  • Ask only what you will use. Every question costs someone's time.
  • Test your survey with five people before you send it to everyone.
  • Remove duplicates before you count, for example by email or phone number.
  • Break results down by sex, age and location to see who you are missing.
  • Get consent and explain how the data will be used.
  • Store raw data securely and share only totals, never names or contact details.
  • Write down your method, so next year's numbers can be compared fairly.
  • Note what you can't measure, and say so in your reports.
A consent statement you can adapt: "We are collecting this information to understand how our programme is working and to improve it. Taking part is voluntary, and you can stop at any time. We will keep your answers confidential, and we will only publish totals, never your name or contact details."

5 A learning rhythm for small teams

Every month

Update the numbers. Check activities against the plan. Flag problems early.

Every quarter

Review progress against targets with the team. Agree what to change.

Every year

Look at outcomes, ask participants what changed, and publish an impact report.

After every event or project phase, ask four questions

  1. What did we plan to happen?
  2. What actually happened?
  3. Why was there a difference?
  4. What will we keep, and what will we change next time?
