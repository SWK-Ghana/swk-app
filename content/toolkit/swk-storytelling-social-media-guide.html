Storytelling and Social Media Guide

Tell true stories that move people to act, without taking away anyone's dignity. Content pillars, a post formula, an ethical storytelling checklist and a simple monthly plan.

Free to use and adapt For communications leads and volunteers swkghana.org/support · October 2026

Start with two questions

  • Who do we want to reach: donors, volunteers, partners, the community?
  • What do we want them to do next: register, give, share, partner?

The principle

The people in your stories are partners, not props. If they would be uncomfortable seeing the post, don't publish it.

1 Four content pillars

Rotate between these so your feed shows your impact, your people and your purpose, not only requests.

Impact stories

One person, one change. A participant's journey, in their own words, with their consent.

Behind the scenes

Your team, volunteers and partners at work. Preparing an event, a field visit, a planning meeting.

Useful knowledge

Tips, data and how-tos your audience can use, linked to your cause. These are the posts people save and share.

Calls to action

Register, volunteer, donate, partner. One clear ask per post, with a deadline when there is one.

2 The anatomy of a strong post

HookThe first line earns the next. A surprising number, a question, or a moment.
StoryOne person or one moment, with a concrete detail. Specific beats general.
ImpactWhat changed, in a number or an outcome, and why it matters.
Call to actionOne next step, with a link: "Register at…", "Join us on…".
CreditTag partners and photographers, and add two or three relevant hashtags.
Caption formula: [Hook]. [One-sentence story]. [What changed]. [What you can do: link]. [Thanks and tags].

3 Ethical storytelling checklist

  • Informed consent, in writing, explaining where the story or photo will appear.
  • A parent's or guardian's consent for anyone under 18, and the child's agreement too.
  • Dignity first: no images of distress used to shock, and no "before and after" that shames.
  • Their words, not ours. Quote people accurately and let them describe their own lives.
  • Protect identities where there is any risk: change names, hide faces and locations.
  • Accuracy: numbers checked, and nothing exaggerated for effect.
  • The right to withdraw: take a story down if the person asks.
  • Credit photographers, partners and the communities you work with.

4 Photos, video and accessibility

Better photos with a phone

  • Use natural light, and keep the sun behind you.
  • Shoot at eye level, and get closer than feels natural.
  • Shoot landscape for your website, vertical for stories and reels.
  • Capture action and faces, not only group poses.

Make every post accessible

  • Add alt text that describes each image.
  • Caption every video. Most people watch with the sound off.
  • Don't put important information only inside an image.
  • Use plain language and high-contrast colours.

6 Measure what matters

Once a month, look at four numbers and do more of what works.

  • Reach: how many people saw your posts.
  • Engagement rate: reactions, comments and shares, divided by reach.
  • Clicks: how many people followed your link.
  • Actions: registrations, volunteers or donations that came from social media.
